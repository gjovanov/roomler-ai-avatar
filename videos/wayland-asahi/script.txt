# [The KDE Plasma desktop on the Mac mini, live in a browser tab: the About window, the spinning cube. Full screen.]
goran3: Looks like a local desktop, doesn't it?
goran3: That's a remote Mac mini running Linux and Wayland, and I'm controlling it from this browser tab.
# [The desktop beside the presenter (A).]
goran3: The remote desktop tool is called Roomler. I built it as a result of my wife swearing at TeamViewer.
goran3: Full story of why I built it, I've linked the blog below.
# [Return immediately to the live remote desktop. Drag a window across the screen; enlarge the viewer's frame counter. Full screen.]
goran3: Let's see how it works on Linux Wayland.
goran3: Fifty-six frames per second, on Wayland, from a browser tab.
goran3: Every single frame here is software encoded.
# [Overlay: "Software encoding · 56 FPS". The desktop beside the presenter (B).]
goran3: That's because this Mac has an Apple GPU, but on Asahi Linux there's currently no GPU driver Roomler can use.
goran3: So even though the dedicated hardware is sitting right there, we can't really use it.
goran3: However, we're still getting fifty-six frames per second from the CPU.
# [Overlay: "Earlier build: 27 FPS → Now: 56 FPS". Full screen.]
goran3: Roomler does tell the CPU: while I'm encoding, give me the fast core and the clock I actually need.
# [The frame counter visible. Beside the presenter (A).]
goran3: Once Asahi gets a usable video-encode path, a hundred and twenty frames per second, even a hundred and forty-four, becomes the kind of target worth chasing.
goran3: I haven't measured that on Asahi yet, because, well... first we need the driver.
# [Open a text editor. Full screen.]
goran3: Who cares about FPS? Let's see it in action.
goran3: Let's type something.
# [Type a sentence, correct something. The editor zoomed 2x. Full screen.]
goran3: Mouse movement, typing, editing, it feels local.
goran3: Unfortunately, so does my spelling. Roomler doesn't help with that.
# [The sharing banner at the top of the remote machine, zoomed 2x: viewer identity, timer, Disconnect. Full screen.]
goran3: There's another part I care about just as much. Security, consent versus unattended, transparency when someone is viewing your screen and being able to disconnect the viewer.
goran3: In this setup, Roomler captures that user's desktop only while their session is actually on screen and unlocked.
goran3: The banner tells them who's connected and for how long.
# [Hover over Disconnect, click it. Full screen.]
goran3: Clicking Disconnect, you kick out the viewer and take control away from them.
# [End card: roomler.ai, GitHub. The presenter (B).]
goran3: That's Roomler. It's an open-source, self-hostable remote desktop working on Windows, macOS and Linux Wayland from a browser.
goran3: Try it on your own machines. Break something. Tell me what I missed.
goran3: Talk to you soon.
